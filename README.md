## Why Anti DDoS Game Server Hosting Isn't Optional Anymore

Here's the thing most hosting comparison articles won't tell you: the size and frequency of DDoS attacks against game servers has been climbing steadily. We're not talking about the 3Gbit nuisance floods from a decade ago. Modern attacks routinely hit double-digit Gbit figures, and game servers are prime targets because taking one down is cheap for the attacker and painful for the operator. Minecraft, Rust, CS2, ARK, FiveM/GTA V—basically any title with a public server browser is a sitting duck without proper mitigation.

A regular hosting plan with "some DDoS protection" usually means the provider scrubs traffic *after* it already saturated your uplink. By then, your players are already disconnected. What you actually need is always-on, network-level filtering that scrubs the attack *before* it touches your server—and enough mitigation capacity that the attack doesn't just get passed along to your neighbors on a shared pipe.

That's the gap a purpose-built **anti DDoS game server hosting** setup fills, and it's worth understanding the difference before you commit to a provider.
